Notation, scope, and sources

Hex digits represent four bits; two digits are one byte. ⊕ means bitwise XOR. + in the hash rounds means addition modulo 2³². ROTL / ROTR rotate a 32-bit word; SHR shifts in zeros. Concatenation joins bytes or bits.

This lab demonstrates AES-128 encryption of one block, DES encryption of one block, and complete SHA-256 / MD5 hashing of short UTF-8 messages. “SHA” is a family: this page uses SHA-256, as your syllabus does. SHA-512 has different widths and SHA-3 has a different construction. Internal calculations are extra support; the outline and labeled diagrams are the exam priority.

These are teaching implementations. AES block transforms here do not add a mode, IV, padding, or authentication. DES is obsolete and MD5 has broken collision resistance. A plain hash alone does not authenticate a sender.

Checked against your module, NIST AES specification, archived DES specification, NIST Secure Hash Standard, MD5 specification, and MD5 security update.
